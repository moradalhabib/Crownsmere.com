# Crownsmere.com research notes (October 2026)

Research behind the second round of website improvements, what was changed, and what only the business can supply. Not published on the website (`docs/` is excluded in `_config.yml`).

Sources were web searches in October 2026. Most legal points come from council, university and law-firm summaries of the legislation; GOV.UK could not be opened directly from the research environment. Check anything legal against GOV.UK or with a solicitor before relying on it.

## 1. What changed in the law that affects the site

### Renters' Rights Act 2025 (England): rent in advance
- From **1 May 2026**, landlords and agents may not take rent before a tenancy is signed, and may ask for no more than **one month's rent** between signing and the start date (28 days for shorter rent periods). A refundable holding deposit of up to one week's rent is still allowed. Breaches can be fined up to £7,000.
- University and students' union guidance expects this to end the common workaround for international students without a UK guarantor (paying 6 to 12 months upfront), and expects more landlords to ask for a UK or professional guarantor instead. Paid guarantor services must stay optional.
- University-owned halls and purpose-built student accommodation run by providers signed up to an approved code (ANUK/Unipol, UUK) are treated differently.
- **On the site:** the English and Arabic student pages now open "Good to know" with this change, and why a guarantor matters more. This strengthens the case for Crownsmere's student guarantor help.

Sources: [Guide to the Renters' Rights Act (GOV.UK)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act), [Renters' Rights Act overview for tenants (GOV.UK)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/renters-rights-act-overview-for-tenants), [Explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/26/pdfs/ukpgaen_20250026_en.pdf), [Pinsent Masons guide](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/guides/renters-rights-act-2025-guide-private-landlords-england), [Unipol: headline changes for students](https://www.unipol.org.uk/advice/renters-rights-act-advice-for-students/headline-changes-renters-rights-act/), [University of York](https://www.york.ac.uk/students/accommodation/private-sector/renters-rights-act/), [Royal Holloway SU](https://www.su.rhul.ac.uk/news/article/surhul/-What-the-New-Renters-Rights-Act/), [Osborne Clarke on PBSA](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/what-does-renters-rights-act-mean-purpose-built-student-accommodation-and-other-student), [Forsters on student tenancies](https://www.forsters.co.uk/news-and-views/renters-rights-act-2025-student-tenancies-pbsa).

### What an estate or letting agent's own website must show
- **Letting agents (Consumer Rights Act 2015):** publish all fees (with VAT) on the agent's own website, name the redress scheme they belong to, and say whether they belong to a client money protection scheme. Councils can fine up to £5,000 per breach; failing to join a CMP scheme when holding client money can cost up to £30,000.
- **Estate agents and buying agents:** must belong to an approved redress scheme (The Property Ombudsman or the Property Redress Scheme). The Property Ombudsman has a code of practice specifically for buying agents. Estate agency businesses must register with HMRC for anti-money laundering supervision before trading.
- **Limited companies:** the website must show the registered name, registration number, place of registration and registered office (Company, LLP and Business (Names and Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2015).
- **On the site:** `tools/build.py` now has `BUSINESS` (company and regulatory details) and `FEES`. The trading name (Crownsmere Estate), principal (Alaa Qadir, Senior Property Consultant) and contact address (hello@crownsmere.com) are filled in and show in every footer. The company and regulatory fields and `FEES` are still empty; each appears once filled in, in the footer line or, for fees, as a section on the services page linked from the fees question. **This is the most important owner action below.**
- **Commission only (confirmed by the owner, 23 October 2026):** Crownsmere acts as a middleman and is paid only by commission. Depending on the deal, the landlord or seller pays, or the client does. Crownsmere never holds client money. The owner decided not to describe how Crownsmere is paid on the website. The advisory service no longer calls itself "independent, impartial", since commission can come from the other side of a deal.
- **What that means legally (to check with a solicitor or the redress scheme):** introducing a client to a buyer, seller, landlord or tenant on that client's instructions is still estate or lettings agency work, whoever pays, so redress scheme membership and HMRC anti-money laundering supervision still apply. A commission paid by a landlord or tenant is a fee letting agents must publish with VAT (`FEES`). Trading Standards (NTSELAT) treats referral fees and commissions from third parties as information clients must be told, and a commission from the other side of a deal must be disclosed in writing to the client before work begins. A commission charged to a tenant is likely to be a fee the Tenant Fees Act bans for most tenancies in England (it does not cover company lets or rents over £100,000 a year), so check before charging one. Sources: [NTSELAT referral fees guidance (Propertymark)](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/resource/ntselat-referral-fees-guidance.html), [Estate Agents Act 1979, section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38/section/1/data.html), [Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 83](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/83/data.htm), [Tenant Fees Act statutory guidance (GOV.UK)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tenant-fees-act-amended-by-the-renters-rights-act-2025/tenant-fees-act-2019-statutory-guidance-for-enforcement-authorities), [Property Industry Eye: letting agents' duty to disclose commissions](https://propertyindustryeye.com/letting-agents-duty-disclose-commissions-paid/).

Sources: [Woking Borough Council](https://woking.gov.uk/letting-and-property-agent-responsibilities), [Enfield Trading Standards](https://www.enfield.gov.uk/services/business-and-licensing/trading-standards/letting-agent-information), [Hammersmith & Fulham](https://www.lbhf.gov.uk/business/trading-standards-0/landlords-letting-agents-and-property-management-companies), [The Property Ombudsman: businesses](https://www.tpos.co.uk/businesses/), [TPO buying agents code](https://www.tpos.co.uk/members/news-articles/item/tpo-announces-new-code-of-practice), [Homeflow: start-up agency compliance](https://www.homeflow.co.uk/posts/startup-estate-agencies-understanding-compliance), [Brodies: online trading disclosures](https://brodies.com/insights/corporate/online-trading-disclosures-does-your-company-comply/), [CMS: trading disclosures](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/trading-disclosures-under-the-companies-act-2006-an-overview).

## 2. Market: why Arabic matters
- Beauchamp Estates' 2026 research: buyers from the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait made up about 25% of Mayfair sales and 20% of prime central London sales in the first half of 2026; US and Gulf buyers together were 55% of sales over £15 million. These are agent figures with a commercial interest, but they point the same way as other reports of Gulf buyers returning.
- **On the site:** the Arabic student page now has its own address (`students-ar.html`), linked to the English page with `hreflang` and in the sitemap. Google recommends a separate URL per language; a JavaScript switch on one URL may never show the Arabic version to search engines. Old `students.html?lang=ar` links redirect.
- **Recommended next step (not done):** an Arabic version of the home, services and contact pages for Gulf buyers and sellers, written or reviewed by a native speaker. Quality matters more than speed for a luxury audience, so this should not be machine translated.

Sources: [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/uk-housing-market-news/us-and-gulf-buyers-drive-ultra-prime-property-market-rebound/), [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2026/06/26/steep-property-discounts-lure-gulf-buyers-back-to-london/), [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/articles/lure-of-london-homes-is-hard-to-resist-for-arab-buyers/), [Google guidance quoted in the Renuo SEO checklist](https://open-source.renuo.ch/seo-checklist/Translated_urls.html), [Hreflang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hreflang).

## 3. Trust and conversion
- Guidance for high-value professional services is consistent: put credentials, real testimonials, clear contact details and a plain "what happens next" near the decision point, keep forms short, and label buttons with what happens next. The site already does the last three.
- Buying-agent clients check professional memberships (Propertymark, RICS, The Property Ombudsman) and want written fee terms and references.
- **On the site:** `TESTIMONIALS` in `tools/build.py` adds a "What clients say" section to the home page once real, permitted quotes are added. Nothing invented is shown.
- **Enquiry delivery:** the forms open the visitor's email app. On computers that often opens nothing (no mail app set up), so the "your email is ready" panel now also offers **Open in Gmail** and **Open in Outlook** on computers. Phones keep using their own mail app.

Sources: [ABA GPSolo, 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/gpsolo/resources/ereport/2026-july/ask-techie-what-website-features-actually-convert-visitors-paying-clients), [Zenind: conversion for service businesses](https://www.zenind.com/en-US/help/post/how-to-improve-your-website-conversion-rate-a-practical-guide-for-service-businesses), [Simon Willison: Gmail compose URLs](https://til.simonwillison.net/google/gmail-compose-url), [Microsoft Q&A: Outlook web deep links](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/4619953/want-to-pre-populate-to-cc-subject-body-by-passing).

## 4. Accessibility, speed and search
- **WCAG 2.2 focus not obscured (2.4.11):** sticky headers and fixed bottom bars can hide the control a keyboard user has tabbed to. The page now sets `scroll-padding` so browsers keep focus clear of both; a test tabs through four pages on a phone to check. (A JavaScript fix was tried and removed: it fought smooth scrolling, while the CSS alone works.) Sources: [BOIA](https://www.boia.org/blog/tips-for-meeting-wcag-2.2s-focus-not-obscured-success-criteria), [Vispero](https://vispero.com/prevent-focused-elements-from-being-obscured-by-sticky-headers/).
- **Images:** photographs are now also served as AVIF (304 KB of WebP becomes 206 KB; the home photo 102 KB becomes 70 KB, visually identical), with WebP and JPEG fallbacks.
- **Instant page changes:** Chrome and Edge fetch a page in the background when the visitor hovers or starts to tap a link (Speculation Rules, same-site only). The content security policy allows exactly that block by its hash, so no other inline script can run. Pages also cross-fade in Chrome, Edge and Safari 18.2+ (cross-document view transitions), switched off for visitors who prefer reduced motion. Sources: [MDN script-src](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/Web/HTTP/Headers/Content-Security-Policy/script-src), [Chrome: cross-document view transitions](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions/cross-document).
- **Structured data:** FAQ rich results no longer appear in Google (fully withdrawn in 2026), but the markup is harmless and still helps machines read the page, so it stays. The business markup now lists English and Arabic as enquiry languages. Sources: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results/574429/), [Techwyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/ai-search/google-faq-rich-results-deprecated-2026).
- **Not done, on evidence:** `llms.txt`. Studies across 300,000 domains found no effect on AI citations and AI crawlers rarely fetch it. Sources: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/llms-txt-shows-no-clear-effect-on-ai-citations-based-on-300k-domains/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/llms-txt-adoption-rises-8-8x-but-97-of-files-get-zero-ai-requests/).

## 4b. Search engines (SEO audit, 26 October 2026)
- **Audit:** Lighthouse (mobile) scored every page 100 for SEO, accessibility and best practices and 92 to 98 for performance. Its SEO score only covers basics, so each page was also checked by hand. Problems found and fixed:
  - the Arabic page declared itself English;
  - one title and five descriptions were long enough to be cut off in results;
  - Sell, Invest and Advisory had under 100 words each;
  - each service page had only 2 to 5 links from the rest of the site (the Arabic page had 1);
  - home links pointed at `/index.html`, a duplicate of the canonical `/`;
  - the favicon was 64px (Google asks for a multiple of 48px);
  - `/favicon.ico` was missing;
  - `main.js` held up the first paint.
- **Structured data:** one connected description per page (business, website, page, breadcrumb trail, service, questions). FAQ rich results no longer show in Google, but the markup still tells machines, including AI search, what each page answers. Google uses `WebSite` markup on the home page, including `alternateName`, to choose the site name it shows. Sources: [Google: site names](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/site-names), [Google: favicons](https://goo.gle/3VIh2V3).
- **IndexNow:** Bing's recommended way to hear about new and changed pages; the key is published as a file at the site root. Google does not use it. Sources: [IndexNow guide (third-party)](https://geo.tenten.co/en/blog/indexnow-implementation), [Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow (third-party)](https://geotoolbox.ai/blog/bing-webmaster-tools).
- **Local and AI search:** studies by vendors (directional, not independent) find that a Google Business Profile and consistent directory listings are the most-cited sources for local businesses in AI answers, ahead of the business's own website. A service-area business must hide its address but still needs a real one for verification (not a PO box or virtual office). Sources: [Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137?hl=en-GB), [Beancount: AI Overviews and small businesses](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/16/google-ai-overviews-chatgpt-perplexity-small-business-visibility-guide).
- **Not done:** neighbourhood landing pages (ten near-identical area pages risk being treated as doorway pages unless each has genuinely local content written by someone who knows the area), Arabic versions of the main pages (need a native writer), and `llms.txt` (no measurable effect, see section 4).

## 5. What only the business can supply (in priority order)
1. **Regulatory and company details** in `BUSINESS`: legal name, company number and registered office (if a company), redress scheme and membership number, AML supervisor, VAT number. Client money protection is not needed while Crownsmere holds no client money.
2. **Commission rates** in `FEES` for any commission paid by a landlord or tenant (amount with VAT, or how it is worked out). A legal requirement for letting agents in England.
3. **A professional photograph of Alaa** (`PORTRAIT`) for her profile page, and **verified figures** for `TRACK_RECORD` (for example years in prime London property, or homes found). A real face and real numbers are the strongest trust signals for a one-to-one service.
4. **Real client testimonials** (with permission) in `TESTIMONIALS`, and a Google Business Profile to collect reviews.
5. **Higher-resolution originals** of the photographs (the home photo is 1,055px wide and looks soft on large screens).
6. **Arabic versions of the main pages**, written or reviewed by a native speaker.
7. **Optional:** cookie-free, self-hosted visitor statistics to measure enquiries after the redesign (the privacy notice would need updating first).
